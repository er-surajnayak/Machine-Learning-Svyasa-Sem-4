01 · Concept

What is an Outlier?

📌 An outlier is a data point that behaves very differently from the rest of the data. It does not follow the normal pattern.

Easy Real-Life Example

Suppose marks of students are: 70, 72, 75, 71, 74, 73, 98

Most students scored around 70–75. But one student scored 98.
That 98 behaves differently. Hence, 98 is an outlier.

Relatable Analogy

🏍️ 🏍️ 🏍️ 🏍️ 🏍️
Normal Pattern: A parking lot full of bikes
🚁
The Outlier: A helicopter lands there
🧠 Important Understanding: Outliers are NOT always bad. Sometimes they represent mistakes or fraud, but sometimes they represent rare events or important discoveries (like a patient recovering unusually fast).

03 · Categories

Types of Outliers

There are mainly 3 types of outliers:

A
Global Outlier
A point far away from ALL other points.
Example: Employee salaries are ₹25k, ₹30k, ₹28k, ₹27k. Suddenly someone earns ₹50 crore. That's a global outlier.
B
Contextual Outlier
Depends on the context.
Example: 40°C temperature is normal in summer, but highly abnormal in winter. Same value, different context.
C
Collective Outlier
A group behaving abnormally together.
Example: One failed login is normal. 1000 failed logins in 1 minute is highly suspicious. Together they form collective outliers.

05 · Statistics

Z-Score Method

📌 Main Idea: Z-score measures "How far a point is from the mean," calculated using the standard deviation.
Z = (X - μ) / σ
where: X = data point, μ = mean, σ = standard deviation
Z-scoreMeaning
0Exactly at mean
1Slightly away
2Far
> 3Possible outlier
⚠ Rule: If |Z| > 3, the point is usually considered an outlier.

Limitation of Z-Score

Z-score works best when data follows a normal distribution.

Problem: Extreme values affect the mean itself.
Hence, Z-score may fail for skewed datasets.

06 · Statistics

IQR (Interquartile Range) Method

⭐ VERY IMPORTANT for exams! Also very practical.
📌 Main Idea: Instead of mean, IQR uses quartiles and median. Because median is more stable and less affected by extreme values.

Quartiles & Formula

Q1
25% position
Q2
Median (50%)
Q3
75% position
IQR = Q3 - Q1
Lower limit = Q1 - 1.5 * IQR
Upper limit = Q3 + 1.5 * IQR
Any value outside this range becomes an outlier.

Example

Dataset: 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 100

  • Suppose Q1 = 3 and Q3 = 7.
  • IQR = 7 - 3 = 4
  • Upper limit = 7 + 1.5(4) = 13

Since 100 > 13, 100 is an outlier.

08 · Clustering Concepts

Distance-Based Outlier Detection

📌 Main Idea: If a point is very far from other points, then it may be an outlier.
👨👨👨👨👨
Normal Crowd
🧍
The Lonely Outlier

Distance Formula

Usually Euclidean distance is used.

d = √((x₂ - x₁)² + (y₂ - y₁)²)

Connection with Clustering

Clustering groups similar nearby points. Outliers do not belong strongly to any cluster and remain isolated.

Example in K-Means: Sometimes one point lies very far from centroid. That point distorts the centroid and reduces clustering quality. Hence, outlier removal is often done before clustering.

09 · Advanced Algorithm

DBSCAN — Density-Based Clustering

Full form: Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise

🧠 Why DBSCAN? K-Means needs K beforehand, is sensitive to outliers, and assumes circular clusters. DBSCAN solves these issues.

Main Idea

Instead of asking "Which centroid is closest?", DBSCAN asks "Where are points densely packed together?"

  • Dense regions become: ✅ Clusters
  • Sparse isolated regions become: ✅ Noise / Outliers

Core Concepts in DBSCAN

1
Epsilon (ε)
Neighborhood radius. "How far should we search around a point?"
Analogy: WiFi range. Inside range = connected, outside = disconnected.
2
MinPts
Minimum nearby points required to form a dense region.
3
Core Point
A point having ≥ MinPts neighbors within ε distance. Forms the center of clusters.
4
Border Point
A point having < MinPts neighbors but lies near a core point. Edge members of cluster.
5
Noise Point
A point not near a dense region; isolated. ✅ Outlier / Noise
Point TypeCondition
Core Point≥ MinPts neighbors
Border Point< MinPts but near core
Noise PointIsolated (Outlier)

11 · Practice

Quiz Yourself

Score: 0 / 6 correct

Q1. Why are outliers removed before K-Means clustering?
Correct! Outliers significantly affect the mean, pulling centroids away from the actual dense areas, leading to poor clusters.
Q2. Are all outliers bad data points?
Correct! While they can be mistakes, outliers in areas like healthcare or fraud detection are exactly the important events we want to find.
Q3. Why is DBSCAN a powerful clustering algorithm?
Correct! DBSCAN is density-based, allowing it to find irregularly shaped clusters and isolate noise naturally.
Q4. Why is IQR preferred over Z-score for skewed data?
Correct! The median and quartiles are robust statistics, making IQR reliable even when extreme outliers are present.
Q5. What is a Global Outlier?
Correct! A global outlier is universally distant from all other observations across the entire dataset.
Q6. In the DBSCAN algorithm, what characterizes a "Noise Point"?
Correct! Noise points do not meet the MinPts requirement and are not reachable from any core point. They are the outliers.
